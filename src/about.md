Alice Hamilton Farmer is an **Associate Professor of Law at the University of Maine** and a
**Senior Adviser for Climate Rights International**. For over a decade she was a lawyer
with UNHCR, the UN Refugee Agency, in offices around the world including the United States.

Across twenty years she has worked on refugee protection and the rights of people in
displacement, in the field and in the courts. Her research now asks how climate change is
reshaping the international legal framework of forced displacement, with a focus on
small-scale fishing communities and other coastal populations living with slow-onset loss.

Until January 2024 she was head of the United States legal team for **UNHCR**, where she
oversaw strategic litigation and policy analysis, drafted the agency's legal positions, and
represented it before the US Supreme Court and the federal courts of appeals. She was also
UNHCR's focal point for climate change, and led its work on access to counsel for people
seeking asylum. Her field service with UNHCR includes deployment as Senior Protection
Officer in Chişinău, Moldova at the onset of the Ukrainian refugee emergency; refugee
status determination across the Caribbean; and protection cluster lead in Liberia.

Before UNHCR she worked as an international human rights lawyer for **Human Rights Watch**,
the **ACLU**, and the **Norwegian Refugee Council**. At Human Rights Watch she created and
led the organisation's global campaign to end the immigration detention of children,
carrying out field investigations in more than twelve countries. Her research took her into
detention centres and refugee camps in Malta, Indonesia, Greece, Italy and Thailand, and
into public schools across the American South.

She was a Fellow at the **Center for Advanced Study in the Behavioral Sciences** at
Stanford and a Visiting Fellow at the **Refugee Studies Centre**, University of Oxford. She
is building a climate change law lab, and is co-editing *Climate Change, Migration, Gender,
and the Law*, forthcoming from Edward Elgar.

## Teaching

She has lectured on human rights at Harvard Law School, Georgetown University Law Center,
and the Institut d'études politiques de Paris (Sciences Po).
